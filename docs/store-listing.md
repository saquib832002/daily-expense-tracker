# Play Store listing copy

Paste-ready text for the three fields Play asks for, with the limits it
enforces. Character counts are given because Play rejects an over-length field
rather than truncating it, and because a short description that runs to 81
characters is a five-minute mystery.

---

## App name — max 30 characters

```
Daily Expense Tracker
```
**21 characters.**

Deliberately plain. Play's policy is explicit about keyword stuffing and
decorative characters in titles, and a name like *"Expense Tracker: Budget,
Money Manager, Bills"* is both against the rules and the mark of an app nobody
trusts. The keywords belong in the description, where they are allowed.

If the exact name is taken, the next options in order:

```
Expense Tracker — Daily Log     (27)
Daily Spend Tracker             (19)
```

---

## Short description — max 80 characters

```
Track daily expenses offline, scan bills, back up to your own Google Drive.
```
**75 characters.**

This is the line people actually read: it sits under the icon in search
results, and for most installs it is the *only* text they see. So it names the
three things that distinguish this app rather than describing the category —
**offline**, **scanning**, and **your own Drive** — and it avoids the four words
every competitor uses in the same slot (simple, easy, powerful, best).

Alternatives, same length budget:

```
Expenses, budgets and bill scanning — private, offline, on your phone.   (70)
Daily expense tracker with bill scanning, budgets and Drive backup.      (67)
```

## Full description — max 4000 characters

```
Record what you spend in a couple of seconds, see where the money actually goes, and keep every rupee of it on your own phone.

Daily Expense Tracker is a complete personal finance diary — expenses, income, budgets, bills and reports — built to be fast enough to use at the counter and private enough to trust with the details.

REMINDERS THAT ARE ACTUALLY USEFUL

• A note when a warranty is about to run out
• A warning if your backup has not run for a fortnight

Everything is worked out on your phone. There are no marketing notifications and no way for us to send you one.

MORE THAN EXPENSES

Signing in with Google is required so that backup works from the first day — it is what protects your history when a phone is lost or replaced. The developer receives none of your data.

BILL PHOTOS NEVER LEAVE THE PHONE

Free, with no advertising.
```

**About 2,900 characters.** Play allows 4,000, and filling that is a mistake:
the listing page shows roughly the first three lines before *Read more*, and
almost nobody expands it. Everything that matters is in the first paragraph.

## Notes on why it reads this way

**The first 160 characters do the work.** Play collapses the description after
about three lines, so the opening sentence has to be the whole pitch. Bullet
lists below it are for the minority who expand — and for Play's own indexing,
which reads the text for search terms.

**The honest paragraphs are not filler.** The two hardest-won lines in this
listing are the ones about the mandatory Google sign-in and about ML Kit's
diagnostics. Both are things a reviewer could otherwise call a surprise, and
both are things a user would find out anyway. Stating them here is what makes
the rest of the privacy claims believable — and the data-safety form has to say
the same thing, so the two must not disagree.

**"What it does not do" prevents one-star reviews.** Half the one-star reviews
on manual expense trackers are *"doesn't connect to my bank"*. Saying so in
advance turns a disappointed installer into someone who never installed, which
is worth more than the install.

**No competitor names, no superlatives.** Play's policy forbids referencing
other apps in your listing, and "best" or "#1" without evidence invites a
takedown. Nothing here needs either.

**Keywords are present but not stuffed:** expense tracker, budget, bills,
receipts, spending, personal finance, money, warranty, loyalty cards, currency
converter — each appears where it belongs in a sentence a person would read.
